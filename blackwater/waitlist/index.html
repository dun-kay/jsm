A glimpse inside Blackwater Bay

Jules rubbed her thumb along the edge of her skirt.

“But it doesn’t say they found any of them,” Owen said. “See, there are more articles. Search continued… Search abandoned… Families appeal for help… then nothing.”

Laura thought for a moment, then began slowly. “So four teenagers vanish twenty-five years ago. Without a trace or any explanation. And then we saw one of them standing on the beach on Sunday night.”

“Allegedly,” Kit said. “Owen and I didn’t actually see him up close, remember.”

“It was him!”

“You saw him through fifty metres of fog.”

“So did Jules.”

Kit looked at Jules. She stopped fidgeting for a moment. “It was him.”

A shadow fell across the photograph. “Sorry. Am I interrupting something?” Laura looked up. It was the new boy. Close up, she noticed his dark hair had loose curls that framed his face.

“Well that doesn’t make any sense,” said Laura.

“I told you they were in the walls.”

Behind them Jules began making a sort of gasping noise. Owen felt her hand digging into his shoulder. “Guys… there’s a… a…” she didn’t have to finish her sentence. Standing in the doorway of the room was a woman. She was blackened with ash and impossibly, horribly burned. Tattered rags hung off her bony arms. She slowly opened her mouth, then let out a terrible wail, turned and ran back down the hallway.

“Get after her!” Laura shouted as she bolted through the doorway, Kit close behind her. The woman was already halfway down the corridor, her tattered clothes whipping behind her as she ran.

“Stop!” Laura yelled. “We just want to talk to you!” The woman glanced back, her burned face catching briefly in the daylight, then disappeared around the corner. Laura pushed harder. She could hear the others thundering behind her, Kit shouting something she couldn’t make out. They rounded the corner and skidded to a halt. The corridor ended abruptly where the fire had destroyed the eastern side of the hotel. The floor ahead had collapsed completely, leaving a gaping hole down into the rooms below. The woman was gone.

“No way,” Kit panted. “Where did she go?”

Laura rushed into the closest room. Empty. Owen checked the other. “Nothing here!”

“She couldn’t have jumped,” Jules said, arriving behind them. She peered cautiously over the collapsed floor. “She’d be dead.”

The Blackwater Four stood together outside the hotel. Daniel and Michael at the back, Evelyn and Sarah in front. They looked happy, just a bunch of kids. Nothing like the sad school photographs that had accompanied every article about their disappearance. Laura stared at it. There was still something strange about the photo. She sat up and switched on her bedside lamp. Daniel. Evelyn. Michael. Sarah. Laura frowned, then grabbed her phone.

She opened the photographs she had taken at the hotel and began flicking through them. The Four at the harbour. At the lighthouse. Sitting on the cliffs. Then, all of a sudden, she realised— “Who took all these photos?” Someone had been there with them, someone close enough that Evelyn pulled faces at them behind the camera, that Daniel goofed off for them. Another member of the group, who, as far as Laura could tell, had never once been mentioned in any of the articles about the Blackwater Four.

Laura lay back, her mind finally slowing now she had figured out what had been bothering her all along. Tomorrow she would tell the others at school. She closed her eyes, the photo still curled gently in her hand. This time, she fell fast asleep.

Chapter 1

The Drowned Boy

It was an unusually cold evening for summer. Rain clouds hung heavy on the horizon, and a dense sea mist had rolled across the bay in the sort of way that could only mean something was wrong.

Not that it seemed to dampen the spirits of the party already rolling along quite nicely on the beach. Shouts and music drifted up towards them through the mist. There must have been fifty people down there already, most gathered around the fire or sitting in little groups along the sand. Beyond them, a light flashed once from the old lighthouse and vanished behind the fog.

Ah, the annual Blackwater High end-of-summer party.

Laura looked over at Jules and smiled. Jules looked nervously back at her, as usual. She had been complaining about the weather since they left home and was now staring suspiciously at the clouds.

“Come on. Let’s get down there. Can’t let the boys have all the fun.”

Jules groaned. “Fine. But I’m not drinking this time.”

“We’ll see.” Laura spotted two familiar shapes beside the bonfire. “Look, the boys are just over there.”

They picked their way down the sand towards them, passing a group already shouting the words to some song Laura didn't recognise.

“Hi, boys.”

Kit gave Laura that annoying look of his. Owen cocked an eyebrow. They were both obviously enjoying themselves a little too much.

“Howdy, girls. Having fun yet?” Kit asked, although, without waiting for their reply, his attention was already wandering towards someone behind them.

“No thanks to you, Kit. We’ll all be soaked in ten minutes. Look at those clouds!”

“Oh, relax, Jules. You don't get summer days as nice as this very often.” Owen shoved a bottle towards her. “Here. Have some.”

Jules wrinkled her nose. “What even is that?”

“Don't know.”

“Greattt.”

Laura snorted and walked over to Kit, kicking him playfully in the shin. “So, Kit. Had a good summer then?”

“Hm. It was fine. Not as good as last.”

“Oh, really? You liked the life of an amateur detective?”

A smile crept across Kit's face as he remembered the mysterious events that had interrupted their lives last summer. He grabbed Laura's hand in an exaggerated handshake. “Well, you see, my dear Watson, it's just because you really are the best detective I know.”

Laura put a hand to her lips in mock surprise, then forgot whatever clever response she'd been planning. As stupid as it sounds, she realised Kit had never actually held her hand before.

Owen sauntered over and punched Kit in the arm. Hard. “This looks serious.” He glanced at their joined hands. “You know Macy’s looking for you, Kit.”

Kit dropped Laura's hand. “Really? Macy Buyers?”

“Not really,” Owen said.

Laura burst out laughing. Macy Buyers, prom queen, little miss perfect.

“Oh, far out, Owen.” Kit buried his face in his hands.

“Everything all right, guys?” Jules asked, watching the horizon. Thunder rolled somewhere out over the water.

“Enough with the rain, Jules. Did you drink that yet?”

“Everything's fine,” Laura said. “We were just talking, you know… mysteries.”

That immediately got Jules's attention. “Oooh.”

Owen leaned closer. “Got another one?”

“Well?”

Laura hesitated. She'd been waiting for the right moment to bring it up. She hadn't really decided there was anything to investigate yet. “Maybe. You know that Mercer kid who's missing?”

“Ben? From our year?” Jules asked. “You know something?”

“No. But everyone's talking about it.”

Everyone was. Blackwater High wasn't big enough for someone to just disappear. Laura had heard three different versions already. By Monday there would probably be ten more. “His bike turned up near the cliffs three days ago,” Laura said. “But no Ben.”

“Maybe he ran away?” Kit said.

“That's what his mum thinks. His dad lives in the city.”

“So what's the mystery?”

“I don't think he did.”

“Why?”

Laura shrugged. “I've met him a couple of times. He's terrified of everything. And if he wanted to run away to the city, why leave his bike at the cliffs?”

Jules looked less convinced. “Maybe someone picked him up?”

“Maybe.”

“So technically there isn't a mystery.”

“Not yet.”

The gang looked at each other.

Owen rubbed his hands together, breaking the silence. “Well. That sounds great.”

Jules glared at him. “Great?”

“Well, not great, great. More like… a great mystery.”

“Yeah, gee, Owen,” Kit said solemnly, mocking him. “How could you be so insensitive? Someone is missing here.”

“Oh, shut up.”

Kit continued laying it on, but Laura had stopped listening. Something had caught her eye by the water, just on the edge of the mist. There was a boy, standing alone. His clothes clung tightly to his body and his hair was soaked flat against his forehead. But nobody would be swimming tonight. Not in this cold. He just stood there facing the dark water as though they didn’t exist. Then he slowly turned his head and looked straight at her.

“Look.” Nobody heard. “Look! Over there. Through the mist. Can you guys see that?”

Owen turned. “See what?”

“The boy. By the water.”

“Oh, great,” Kit said. “Laura, you've already spooked yourself before we've even begun.”

“No, shut up. I'm serious. Look closely. There's someone there. He looked right at me.”

Jules peered into the mist, then grabbed Laura's arm. “Oooh! I see him!”

“Where?” Owen asked.

“There!”

Jules stared at the figure. “Gosh, you don't think…”

Laura looked at her, then said slowly, “That looks a bit like Ben.”

Laura looked back towards the water. She'd only seen Ben a few times, mostly around school and once in the shop. She couldn't be certain, but Jules was right. It looked enough like him.

The boy turned and began walking down the beach towards the cliffs.

“Hey!” Laura took off after him.

“Laura! Nooo… please be careful!” Jules watched her disappear into the mist. “Oh, damn it.” Then she ran after her.

Kit and Owen watched them go.

“Well,” Owen said. “That was unexpected.”

Kit glanced back at the party. The bonfire was warm, there were drinks, and Macy Buyers was somewhere over there. Then he looked towards the mist. “We should probably go after them.”

“You're right. Letting them chase a stranger into the fog does seem like a terrible idea.”

Together, they sprinted after the girls. As they ran, the first drops landed cold on the back of Kit's neck. Then the sky opened properly. Within seconds his shirt was stuck to his shoulders as he and Owen ran down the beach. The party disappeared behind them, swallowed by the mist and swirling rain until there was nothing left but a faint orange glow.

“Laura! Jules!” Nothing.

Kit slowed. The beach looked completely different already. The cliffs rose black on one side and the sea disappeared into grey on the other. In front of them there was nothing but sand and fog. “Where could they have gone?”

Without thinking, he reached down, picked up a large flat stone and hurled it blindly into the mist.

Owen threw up his arms. “What did you do that for?”

“Shh.”

A second later—

CRASH.

“Hey!” a girl's voice shouted somewhere far to the right. “What was that?!”

Kit looked pleased, if not a little surprised. “Laura? Jules? Is that you?”

The girls emerged from the mist. “Yeah, it's us,” Laura said. “What was that noise?”

“No idea. I'm glad we found you.”

Owen shook his head. “Did you find the kid?”

“Not yet. We lost him. He was right in front of us and then—”

“Wait,” Jules said. She pointed upward. “There, look. Isn’t that…” High above them, at the top of the cliff, stood the boy, impossibly close to the edge.

“He's going to jump!” Kit shouted.

“Don't be stupid.”

Laura scanned the edge of the cliff. It rose almost vertically from the beach, disappearing into the fog. There was a walking track somewhere along the top, but she couldn't understand how he'd reached it so quickly. They'd only lost sight of him for a minute, maybe less. She cupped her hands around her mouth. “Hey! You there! What do you want? Who are you?”

The boy didn't answer. The rain hissed harder against the sand as another gust of wind swept in from the sea, obscuring the cliff for just a second.

Laura searched the clifftop. “Wait, where did he go?” There had to be an explanation. A path she couldn't see. A gap in the rocks. They'd been so close.

“He's gone,” Jules whispered.

“Well,” Owen said, “that's that then. Time to go home.”

“I agree,” Jules said immediately. “That's enough excitement for one day. ”

Laura was already walking towards the water, scanning the beach. “That can't be it. No way. Come on… there must be a clue around here somewhere.” After a minute of searching, she saw the tyre tracks. They cut deeply across the wet sand before disappearing around the rocks beneath the cliffs, fresh enough that the rain hadn't yet washed away their edges.

“Guys, look! Tracks, going towards the rocks.”

“Crap,” Kit said. “She's right, let’s go.”

“Of course she is,” Jules muttered, following.

They rounded the rocks and stopped. A car sat wedged between two boulders beneath the cliff, its nose jammed against the rocks, incoming waves nudging against the door panels.

“It must have come off the road above,” Owen said.

“And recently too,” Jules said. “Look, no rust or barnacles.”

Kit scrambled down. “This requires further investigation.”

He splashed through the water and peered through the driver's window. “There's no one in it, come on!”

A minute later all four of them were standing around the vehicle, the waves lapping their knees.

“So what do we do now?” Jules asked. “The tide's coming in pretty fast.”

“Get out before we get any more wet?” Owen suggested, shivering.

“Shut up, you two.” Kit wiped the rain from his face and looked at the rear window. “I’m just trying to think.”

“A difficult process for you is it, Kit?”

“No, no, no, no. I know this car.”

“You know this car? The one we just found suspiciously tossed into the water under a cliff.”

Kit wasn’t listening. He clapped his hands together. “Troy! Troy Mercer drives a black Safari wagon.”

Laura waded around the back. “Troy? As in Ben Mercer's brother?”

Kit nodded. “That’s the one. Do you think we should check out the inside? Looks like there’s a bag in there.”

“Oh, Troy Mercer. And there is a backpack in there. Yes, this is all completely normal,” Owen parroted.

A dark schoolbag lay on the rear seat, just above the waterline. Something had been written across it in thick black marker.

BEN MERCER.

Owen peered through the glass. “Well. That's probably not good.”

Kit grabbed the rear door and pulled. It wouldn't move. Laura joined him, then Owen.

“One, two—” They heaved. The door barely shifted a few centimetres.

“Again.”

It gave way with a rush of seawater. Kit staggered backwards into the water, his shirt now soaked through and plastered against his shoulders.

Laura stared at him.

“What?” Kit said.

“Nothing." She turned away quickly, climbing half inside the car to pull the backpack from the rear seat.

“Laura!” Jules squeaked. “Careful!”

“Got it.” Laura held up the bag.

“Thank God,” Jules said. “Now can we please get out of here before someone sees us?”

A pair of headlights appeared on the road above. “Too late,” Kit said.

A car door slammed. “Quick, behind the rocks.” They splashed through the water and scrambled behind a cluster of rocks beneath the cliff, Laura clutching Ben's backpack against her chest. Seconds later, a flashlight appeared through the mist. Someone was coming down the path.

They squeezed tighter together behind the rocks as the beam moved past them towards the wreck. The stranger waded straight into the rising tide. “Do we have to save him if he drowns?” Kit whispered. Jules elbowed him. The stranger didn't inspect the wreck or look around. He went straight to the rear door they'd opened and climbed inside. His flashlight swept across the back seat, then the floor. Laura looked down at the backpack. The others did the same. Whoever this was knew what he was looking for. The stranger climbed from the car and turned towards the beach. Tall. Dark jacket. Hood pulled low. Kit shifted his foot.

CRUNCH.

The flashlight stopped. Its beam swept across the water, across the rocks— right to where they were hiding.

“Run.” They exploded from their hiding place. Laura hit the sand first, Jules beside her and the boys crashing after them. Behind them the flashlight bounced closer.

“Get back to the party!” The bonfire glimmered ahead through the rain. Music grew louder. Shapes appeared in the mist. Jules stumbled, Kit stopping to pull her up as they kept running, until finally…

People.

They crashed into the edge of the party, pushing between students until they were surrounded by bodies. Laura looked back. The figure stood beyond the party, almost exactly where the boy had stood earlier. He didn't come any closer. As if unwilling to risk being seen, or recognised.

Someone stumbled into Laura, knocking her off balance for a moment. “Sorry!” When she had recovered, the figure was gone.

The four stood among the continuing party, alive again after the rain. Music and bodies pounded around them. Kit looked at the backpack. “Well? Are we going to open it?”

“Not here.” Jules said. “This way.”

They found a quiet spot beneath the old timber stairs leading up from the beach. Laura crouched and pulled the zipper open. There were four things inside: a textbook, homework, a wallet and a phone. Laura opened the wallet. Cash. Bank card. School ID.

Kit frowned. “If he ran away to the city, why leave all this here?”

Laura looked towards the cliffs. Ben had been there, she was sure of it. His brother's car had somehow ended up in the water. And now someone was looking for him, not in a good kind of way.

Owen held out his hand. “Phone.”

“Why do you get the phone?”

“I called it. Now gimme.”

They crowded around him. He held down the power button. The screen flickered, went black, then came to life. The lock screen was covered in notifications.

Messages from friends, his mum, Troy, voicemails. Numbers they didn't recognise.

Owen scrolled. “Police.”

“So they are looking for him,” Kit said.

“Obviously they're looking for him.”

Laura took the phone. The last activity was from three nights ago.

“That's when he disappeared.”

Jules hugged her arms around herself. “Can we give this to the police now?”

“We will.”

“That's not what I asked.”

“Just give us two minutes.”

“Laura—”

Owen leaned over her shoulder. “See if you can unlock it.”

“Weird, no password.” Laura flicked through the phone and found the photos app; scrolling through hundreds of screenshots, friends, a dog, selfies. Suddenly, Kit pointed at the screen.

“Wait, go back! Up, up. That one. Click on that one.”

It was the final photograph, which at first glance appeared just to be black, but was actually a grainy image of the beach at night. Dark water stretched across most of the image, with a little moonlight breaking through the clouds. It was badly framed, as though Ben had taken it quickly.

“That's it?” Kit asked.

Jules took the phone. She spread two fingers across the photograph and enlarged it, moving slowly along the line where the dark water met the sand. There was someone standing near the water. He was barely more than a shape in the original photograph, but, enlarged, they could make out his clothes, his hair, the strange way he stood alone at the edge of the water.

Laura recognised him. “No way.”

Kit leaned closer. “That's him?”

“The kid from tonight?” Owen asked.

Jules handed the phone back.

Laura checked the date on the photo. “No. This doesn't make sense. Ben took this three days ago.”

“So?”

Laura looked at them. “So we followed that boy tonight.” The party seemed farther away now. Beneath the music Laura could hear the waves breaking steadily against the beach.

Nobody said anything for a moment.

Jules was staring at the phone.

“What?” Laura asked.

“Nothing.”

“What?”

Jules took the phone again and enlarged the boy until his face blurred into pixels. “He just looks familiar.”

Kit leaned over. “Familiar how?”

“I don’t know.”

“Well, we did just spend half an hour chasing him.”

“No. I mean before tonight.”

“From school?”

Jules shook her head slowly. “I don't know. My grandma has all these old photographs at her house. Hundreds of them. Blackwater when she was young. School photos, dances, football teams. She makes me look at them every time I visit.”

Owen nodded gravely. “Sounds thrilling.”

She ignored him. “There’s this one boy in some of them. Well, he looks like that, like the boy from tonight.”

Laura looked at the screen. “How much like that?”

“I don't know. Similar.”

“Did she ever tell you his name?”

Jules frowned, trying to remember.

“Daniel something. Cross, maybe?”

“Maybe?”

Owen shrugged. “Could be his dad.”

“Or his uncle,” Kit said.

“Or just someone who looks like him.”

Jules nodded. “Probably.”

Laura took the phone back. That was probably all it was. “Why did you remember him at all?” she asked.

Jules had started picking at the cuff of her jumper again. “Because Grandma told me a story about him once. I don't remember all of it, but she told me that he and some friends from Blackwater High went missing while my mum was at school. The story went that they all, well, they all drowned.”

Owen looked at the photograph again. “And?”

Jules shrugged. “And nothing. That's all I remember.”

Kit looked at the photograph again. “Well, maybe he didn't drown.”

“Or maybe it's not him,” Owen said.

Laura looked once more at the blurry figure standing beside the water. “Jules, see if you can find that photo at your grandma’s.”
