A glimpse inside Blackwater Bay

Jules rubbed her thumb along the edge of her skirt.

“But it doesn’t say they found any of them,” Owen said. “See, there are more articles. Search continued… Search abandoned… Families appeal for help… then nothing.”

Laura thought for a moment, then began slowly. “So four teenagers vanish twenty-five years ago. Without a trace or any explanation. And then we saw one of them standing on the beach on Sunday night.”

“Allegedly,” Kit said. “Owen and I didn’t actually see him up close, remember.”

“It was him!”

“You saw him through fifty metres of fog.”

“So did Jules.”

Kit looked at Jules. She stopped fidgeting for a moment. “It was him.”

A shadow fell across the photograph. “Sorry. Am I interrupting something?” Laura looked up. It was the new boy. Close up, she noticed his dark hair had loose curls that framed his face.

“Well that doesn’t make any sense,” said Laura.

“I told you they were in the walls.”

Behind them Jules began making a sort of gasping noise. Owen felt her hand digging into his shoulder. “Guys… there’s a… a…” she didn’t have to finish her sentence. Standing in the doorway of the room was a woman. She was blackened with ash and impossibly, horribly burned. Tattered rags hung off her bony arms. She slowly opened her mouth, then let out a terrible wail, turned and ran back down the hallway.

“Get after her!” Laura shouted as she bolted through the doorway, Kit close behind her. The woman was already halfway down the corridor, her tattered clothes whipping behind her as she ran.

“Stop!” Laura yelled. “We just want to talk to you!” The woman glanced back, her burned face catching briefly in the daylight, then disappeared around the corner. Laura pushed harder. She could hear the others thundering behind her, Kit shouting something she couldn’t make out. They rounded the corner and skidded to a halt. The corridor ended abruptly where the fire had destroyed the eastern side of the hotel. The floor ahead had collapsed completely, leaving a gaping hole down into the rooms below. The woman was gone.

“No way,” Kit panted. “Where did she go?”

Laura rushed into the closest room. Empty. Owen checked the other. “Nothing here!”

“She couldn’t have jumped,” Jules said, arriving behind them. She peered cautiously over the collapsed floor. “She’d be dead.”

The Blackwater Four stood together outside the hotel. Daniel and Michael at the back, Evelyn and Sarah in front. They looked happy, just a bunch of kids. Nothing like the sad school photographs that had accompanied every article about their disappearance. Laura stared at it. There was still something strange about the photo. She sat up and switched on her bedside lamp. Daniel. Evelyn. Michael. Sarah. Laura frowned, then grabbed her phone.

She opened the photographs she had taken at the hotel and began flicking through them. The Four at the harbour. At the lighthouse. Sitting on the cliffs. Then, all of a sudden, she realised— “Who took all these photos?” Someone had been there with them, someone close enough that Evelyn pulled faces at them behind the camera, that Daniel goofed off for them. Another member of the group, who, as far as Laura could tell, had never once been mentioned in any of the articles about the Blackwater Four.

Laura lay back, her mind finally slowing now she had figured out what had been bothering her all along. Tomorrow she would tell the others at school. She closed her eyes, the photo still curled gently in her hand. This time, she fell fast asleep.
