Chapter 1 Preview

The Disappearance

“Jesus, Laura, will you shut up?” Johnno stopped abruptly and swung round to face her. She clicked off the tape recorder and raised her eyebrows at him. For a moment, there was only the soft rustling of the paperbark trees above them. “You’re not Dale Cooper and this isn’t Twin Peaks, all right? If Ty Senior catches us following him, we’re going to be in serious trouble.” Johnno looked wide-eyed and pale in the moonlight, his red hair damp against his forehead.

Laura smiled at him, holding out her free hand.

He stared at it. “What?”

“For when you get scared.”

Johnno looked down. “I’m not scared, all right? It’s just that—”

But she was already gone, ducking beneath the low branches and moving towards the water. By the time he caught up, she had raised the recorder to her lips again. “Detective Laura Harris resuming her report. It’s nearly midnight on the first of September 1994. Conditions on the southern edge of the Dawesville Estuary are clear, with a full moon providing excellent visibility. We are currently pursuing the suspect, Mr Daniel Ty—”

“Will you at least whisper?” he hissed.

She lowered her voice. “—who is approximately fifty metres offshore and appears to be fishing.”

“There’s no way he’s out here just fishing,” Johnno shot back. “I’m ninety-nine percent sure that man over there murdered Andrew Stanton. Ty Senior was the last person seen with him. What more do you need?”

“Evidence,” Laura said. “Ideally in the form of a body. Now hurry up. We don’t want to miss anything.”

Johnno clicked his tongue, then reached into his bag and handed her a pair of binoculars. He pulled out a heavy camera and raised it to his eye.

Murderer or not, Ty Senior worked with a practised hand. Again and again, his line hissed out across the estuary. By the time Johnno finally nudged her, Laura’s legs had gone numb and the cold had seeped through her jacket.

“Get ready.”

Ty Senior placed the rod on the floor of the dinghy and reached for the motor. It spluttered to life. The boat glided towards a line of yellow buoys about fifteen metres from their hiding place under the trees. At that distance, Laura could clearly see his black sweater and the gold lightning bolt across his red cap.

“What’s he doing?” Johnno breathed.

He cut the engine, leaned over the bow and caught the first line with a gaff, hauling something towards the surface.

“Crab pots. But they can’t be his. He was banned from crabbing last year—it was in the Herald.”

With his back towards them, he lifted the mesh flap of the pot, pushed something wrapped in a black plastic bag inside, then tossed the pot back into the water.

“You don’t think he’s putting bits of Andrew in those?” Johnno asked, grimacing.

As he worked down the line of pots, a loud scream from across the water made them jump.

“What the—” Laura scanned the dark water frantically with her binoculars. “There must be someone out there with him!”

“I didn’t see anyone.” Johnno lowered the camera. The scream came again, this time closer. He looked up as a pale shape swept between the branches above them.

“There! It’s an eastern barn owl. I can’t believe I didn’t recognise the call.” They followed it as it glided over the water, its pale wings flashing in the moonlight before it settled on a branch on the far bank.

“I thought—I thought he was hurting someone.” Laura lowered the binoculars and breathed out. Then she looked back at the dinghy. “Shit.”

“What?”

“He’s gone! Jesus, how did we lose him?” She swept the binoculars across the water. “You check the bank, he might be close!”

Johnno shifted uncomfortably. “Maybe we should get out of here—”

“No, wait.” Laura pointed back towards the dinghy. “Do you see it?”

“See what?”

“If you were going to slip away, don’t you think you’d finish your cigarette first?” A thin curl of smoke rose from a cigarette smouldering on the gunwale.

“So if he didn’t bolt, where did he go?” Johnno asked. Before she could answer, a splash exploded somewhere along the shoreline.

They crashed through the scrub towards the sound, racing onto the sand by the water. Laura stopped so quickly that Johnno almost collided with her.

Dozens of dead fish floated in the shallows, their glassy eyes reflecting the moonlight.

“What could do that?” Johnno whispered. Laura didn’t answer. He grimaced, steadied the camera and took a photograph.

Laura crouched at the water’s edge, studying something caught among the fish. Using a fallen branch, she drew it slowly towards her and onto the sand.

It was a cap—bright red, with a gold lightning bolt across the front. Johnno’s hand trembled as he took another photograph.

Laura raised the tape recorder to her lips. After a moment, she lowered it again.

“I think we need to call Dad.”
